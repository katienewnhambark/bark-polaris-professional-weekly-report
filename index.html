Market context — all Polaris subscription events

Every Polaris trial start, paid activation and trial cancellation across the whole product, not attributed to this journey. These rows count subscriptions where the tables above count emails, so the two do not tie line for line.

  • Data freshness. Both sources are refreshed daily. The banner at the top turns amber when the email source is more than one day behind today.
  • This is not a trial-to-paid rate. The metric counts emails followed by a Polaris plan invoice within 7 days. One converting seller is counted once per qualifying email, and the underlying field is not filtered to paid invoices or to first payments. Treat it as a relative ranking signal between campaigns, not as a conversion rate. The verified market figure sits in the band below.
Definitions — what each number means
  • Journey membership. The campaign group seller_polaris_free_trial_onboarding is not a safe proxy for this journey: six campaigns carrying that group belong to workflow 958363 (the Cleaning/Legal onboarding experiment), and several journey campaigns carried the group seller_marketplace before June. Every campaign here was resolved by workflowId in Iterable and the series is keyed on email_campaign_id, so relabelling in the warehouse cannot shift the totals.
  • Lead-gen sellers are excluded. Invoices - All separates business models cleanly, and lead-gen monetisation (credit and response packs, Elite Pro, Bark Verified) never appears under the polaris_plan invoice family this dashboard reads. Independently, all ten campaigns fire only for sellers who have started a Polaris trial, and top-up invoices across all 2,140 sends are zero.
  • Mailbox groups come from Recipient Domain Group, which is the finest split the source carries — there is no individual-domain field, so gmail.com and googlemail.com both sit under Google. All five groups are listed: Google 1,290 sends, Microsoft 477, Other 325, Apple 31, Yahoo 17. Apple and Yahoo are shown for completeness but 31 and 17 sends across seven months cannot carry an open or click rate — read their counts, not their percentages. Other is a true remainder, with Apple and Yahoo taken out of it. The filter applies to the headline cards and the two email tables. The campaign table and the market-context band are not mailbox-split and keep showing every recipient, so figures will not tie across sections while a mailbox is selected.
  • Deliveries are sends minus bounces, so every rate here already has bounces out of its denominator. Bounces have their own headline card and a row in Email performance. Filtered opens are opens with machine opens removed where the provider can identify them — every open figure and open rate on every tab is the filtered one, there is no unfiltered open rate anywhere on this dashboard.
  • Unique clicks are not available in this source, so click rate is total clicks over deliveries and can exceed 100% in a period where one recipient clicks repeatedly.
  • Plan invoices per 1,000 delivered normalises invoice volume against send volume, so a low-volume trial-ending email is not buried beneath a high-volume one. It is a ranking device, not a rate — see Data quality. It has a headline card and a row in the plan-invoice table, but is not in the campaign table, which carries engagement columns rather than invoice ones.
  • Invoice value is invoice_value_country_local_polaris_plan in AUD, summed over the period. It mirrors invoice_value_country_local in Invoices - All, which is defined as sales value, not invoice_paid_value_country_local. So it is invoiced value, not collected cash, and not a subscription lifetime value. All 2,140 sends are Australia, so local currency is AUD throughout.
  • Welcome-to-paid conversion (plan-invoice table) — welcome (d0) emails delivered, against how many were followed by a Polaris plan invoice within 7 days of the send. It is a genuine per-seller rate because the welcome email fires once per journey entrant, so an email count is a seller count: Tableau's distinct seller count is 364 against 365 sends. Send-dated, so it follows any Period grain. The caret opens the two counts behind it.
  • Trial-to-paid conversion (market table, Month only) — sellers whose Polaris subscription started in the month with Seller is Trial Ever, against how many took a first successful payment (subscription_first_successful_payment_time_utc) within 7 days of their own trial start. The window is per seller, so a trial beginning 28 July runs into August; the column groups sellers by when they trialled, never by when they paid. Its 1,077 sellers reconcile against the 1,081 trial starts in Polaris Subscription Events, and its July figure of 251 matches earlier app-adoption work.
  • Activation MRR is subscription_monthly_value_country_local_activation — the monthly recurring value of every plan activated in the period, in AUD, at the point of activation. It is not billed revenue and takes no account of later cancellation, downgrade or upgrade.
Data quality — what not to trust
  • Why the attributed metric is not trial-to-paid. The fields used are email_count_has_invoice_polaris_plan and invoice_count_polaris_plan from Polaris Email Stats, a source with no reference doc in Bark’s Tableau catalogue and no field-level descriptions. Decomposing every Australian Polaris plan invoice for 11 Feb–6 Sep 2026 in Invoices - All gives 4,129 invoices: 1,014 first paid invoices, 2,706 renewals and 409 unpaid. Only a quarter are first payments and a tenth are unpaid, and nothing in the email source indicates it filters on either dimension. Polaris Email Stats is pre-aggregated with no seller_profile_id, so the journey’s 569 invoices cannot be joined out to first-versus-renewal directly. Those 1,014 first paid invoices do reconcile against the 1,016 paid activations in the market band over the same window and country, which is why activations are the number to trust.
  • Neither conversion rate covers everyone. Welcome-to-paid counts only the 364 sellers the journey welcomed, not all 1,077 who trialled, and its plan invoice is not confirmed as paid rather than merely raised. Trial-to-paid counts every trial seller whether emailed or not, because email receipt is not held against a seller anywhere. The two are not interchangeable, and as printed they cover different date ranges — the journey figure spans February to 6 September, the cohort only the closed months February to July. On the same footing over February to July the journey reads 31.2% (83 of 266 welcomed) against the cohort's 27.1% (211 of 778 trial sellers), a four-point gap with welcomed sellers converting better. Whether that is the email working or selection in who gets welcomed cannot be separated here, since June reached only 15 of 132 trial starters. Cohorts marked (open) show a dash and are excluded, their trials not all having had 7 days to resolve; of 778 closed-cohort sellers 211 paid inside 7 days and 41 later. Cohort months are exact calendar months while the rows above roll weeks up by the month holding each week's Monday, so they differ at month edges, and there is no September column because no week in the data begins in September.
  • Why the email-attributed metric is still not a conversion rate. A real conversion rate needs each activation tied back to the trial it came from. Polaris Subscription Events is a daily event log with no link from an activation to its trial-start date, and neither Seller - All (Polaris) nor Invoices - All carries a Polaris trial-start timestamp that can be paired with a first payment. Dividing activations in a week by trials in that week, or by the week before, would be a lag-shifted approximation rather than a cohort, so it is left out. Over 9 Feb–6 Sep the aggregate does not close either: 1,081 trial starts against 1,016 activations plus 742 trial cancellations, because activations include migrated sellers who never trialled.
  • Open counts are under-recorded from mid-August. Across all Polaris email, machine opens stopped being recorded on 11–12 Aug 2026 and total open volume fell by roughly 84% on unchanged send volume. Filtered opens fell too — from a 5.1–7.6% range across Apr–Jul to 4.8–5.0% since — which machine opens alone cannot explain, since filtered opens exclude them by definition. Clicks per delivery held flat over the same weeks, so the likeliest reading is under-recording rather than sellers disengaging. Cause unconfirmed and it is upstream of the warehouse, visible in Iterable’s own metrics. Compare clicks, not opens, across that boundary. The dashed divider and dagger are still drawn from 10 Aug; the evidence points to 11–12 Aug, and correcting the marker is outstanding.
  • The w/c 11 May 2026 spike in market-context activations (roughly 550, A$91,658 MRR) is the AU1 migration of pre-committed sellers onto paid plans, not organic trial-to-paid conversion. Read the market series either side of it, not through it.
  • Plan split under a profession filter is exact per period, from activations crossed by week, profession and plan. It reconciles four ways: to 432 Starter, 483 Essential, 94 Premium and 7 Bespoke; to each of the twelve professions; to 1,016 overall; and cell by cell against the all-profession plan rows in all 81 populated week-and-plan combinations. Once split three ways the weekly cells are mostly zeros and ones, so the All periods column is the one to read for shape. Activation MRR stays hidden under a profession filter, because value sits on the plan breakdown and not the profession one.
  • Country and profession splits are not available for the journey’s email data. Polaris Email Stats carries country but not seller profession, so the profession dropdown appears only on the market-context table, which comes from a source that does carry it. Activation MRR and the plan split share that constraint and hide themselves when a profession is selected.
  • Week-to-month assignment. The subscription sources are pulled at weekly grain, so month and year views roll weeks up by the month containing each week’s Monday. A week spanning a month boundary counts wholly in the earlier month, and Day view falls back to weeks because a daily split cannot be derived from weekly buckets.
  • Volumes are small. The journey delivered 2,125 emails over seven months — roughly 60 a week across ten campaigns, and a handful on any single day. Campaign-level rates on a Week or Day view move on one or two events. Month or Year is the safer basis for comparing campaigns; Day view is for spotting sends and outages, not rates.
How to read it — controls and layout
  • Mailbox and Period sit in a pinned bar. Both stay at the top of the window as you scroll, so you can change them without going back up. Column headers scroll away with their tables rather than pinning — the tables are only a few rows tall, and a second sticky layer inside a horizontally scrolling container fought with the bar. The first column of each table stays frozen when you scroll sideways. Period is the only time control: the three period tables show one column per period, the campaign table summarises a single period of the same setting, and Year shows the whole run. Only complete periods are used, so a part-finished week never reads as a collapse, and the range actually in use is printed next to each heading.
  • Paging. The three period tables show seven periods each and step through the rest with the arrows at their top right, newest on the right. The arrows on the two email tables move together so the pair stays aligned; the market table pages on its own because it runs on weekly buckets from a different source. The campaign table is shaped the other way round — campaigns are the rows, metrics the columns — so it has no period columns to page across; its arrows move it one whole period at a time instead.
  • The All periods column is not the sum of the seven on screen. It totals the entire range, so it reads the same 2,125 deliveries however you page or change grain. The current unfinished period is marked (part) and is included there.
  • The headline cards do not page. They always report the latest complete period against the one before, and the sparklines always trace the most recent fourteen periods, with their span on each chart’s tooltip. Paging the tables deliberately leaves the cards alone, so the summary stays a fixed reference point.
  • Comparison with the previous period. Tick "Compare with previous period" and every campaign metric carries its change against the period immediately before the one on view — so at Week grain, w/c 31 Aug against w/c 24 Aug, and the arrows move both together. Changes are shown as absolute differences, not percentages: at roughly 60 deliveries a week a move from 3 to 5 is "+2", and calling it "+67%" would dress noise up as a trend. Each cell shows the current figure with the previous period's actual value beneath it — "26.3%" over "▼ was 47.4%" — because reading the earlier number directly beats working it back out of a difference. The arrow gives the direction of the move and the colour says whether that direction is good for the metric, so rising unsubscribes is an up arrow in red while falling unsubscribes is a down arrow in green; an unchanged figure gets no arrow and sits in grey. Hovering any cell spells the whole comparison out, including the size of the change: "Previous week (w/c 24 Aug): 47.4% — now 26.3%, down 21.1pp". A campaign with no sends in the prior period reads "new this period" rather than an infinite increase, and one that has gone quiet reads "no sends". On the oldest page there is nothing to compare against and the heading says so.
  • The delivery floor scales with the period on view — 2 deliveries at Day, 8 at Week, 20 at Month, 25 at Year — because a fixed threshold would empty the table on a single day. Five of the thirty weeks have no campaign above the floor; untick it to see them.
  • The first column of each table is deliberately unlabelled. It lists metric names rather than one metric, so any header there would misdescribe some of its own rows. The table’s subject is in the heading directly above; screen readers get a hidden label.
  • What the plan rows show. They split paid activations by plan only — trials, trial cancellations and MRR are totals. Over 9 Feb–6 Sep, Starter took the most trials (579) but Essential produced more paid activations (483 against 432) and more than three times the activation MRR (A$121,951 against A$38,603). Starter carried the heaviest trial loss at 391 cancellations. Bespoke shows activations and no trials because those sellers were placed on plans directly.
